sync Return to Front
Period: Ancient India
Currency: Karshapana Standard
Type: Imperial Circulation / Trade
Shape & Edge: Irregular Rectangular / Plain

Obverse Design: Features 5 official imperial punch marks stamped individually using iron punches: Sun symbol (imperial solar authority), 6-armed symbol (dynastic crest), 3-arched hill with crescent (Chaitya / royal throne), royal elephant, and a sacred taurine/tree-in-railing motif.

Reverse Design: Typically plain or bearing 1 to 2 minute countermarks known as Shroff Marks (banker test marks) applied during currency exchanges across ancient markets to verify purity.

Historical Event & Context: Issued during the height of the Mauryan Empire under Emperor Chandragupta Maurya and Emperor Ashoka the Great. Hand-cut from beaten silver sheets and punched to exact official weight standards to pay imperial garrisons and facilitate subcontinental pan-Indian trade along the Silk Route.

Period: Republic of India
Currency: Indian Rupee (INR / ₹)
Type: Standard Circulation
Shape & Edge: Circular / Smooth Plain

Obverse Design: Features the Lion Capital of Ashoka Pillar in central focus with national motto "Satyameva Jayate" in Devanagari script below, flanked on left outer ring by "BHARAT" in Hindi and right outer ring by "INDIA" in English along with year date "2015" and Noida solid dot mint mark.

Historical Event & Context: Introduced by the Reserve Bank of India to streamline high-denomination coin usage across retail commerce. Engineered with a Cupro-Nickel center core pressure-locked inside an Aluminum-Bronze outer ring, establishing advanced anti-counterfeiting security for high-velocity daily circulation.
